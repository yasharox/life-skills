
# Grit and Growth Mindset

## 1. Grit

#### Question 1

Grit means having passion and determination to achieve long-term goals, even when facing difficulties or failures. Success is not just about talent or intelligence; continuous effort, patience, and perseverance also play an important role.

## 2. Introduction to Growth Mindset

#### Question 2

A growth mindset is the belief that our skills and intelligence can improve through learning, practice, and effort. Instead of being afraid of challenges or mistakes, we should treat them as opportunities to learn and improve.

## 3. Understanding Internal Locus of Control

#### Question 3

Internal Locus of Control means believing that our actions, decisions, and efforts have an impact on our results rather than depending entirely on luck or external circumstances.

The key point in the video is that we should take responsibility for our actions, focus on things within our control, and recognize that our efforts can create positive changes. This helps us stay motivated and continue working towards our goals.

## 4. How to Build a Growth Mindset

#### Question 4

1. Believe in your ability to figure things out.
2. Question your assumptions.
3. Create your own curriculum for growth.
4. Honour the struggle.

## 5. Mindset - A MountBlue Warrior Reference Manual

A few points to build Growth Mindset as a Software Developer.

#### Question 5

As a software developer, I want to build a growth mindset by continuously learning, practising, and improving my technical and problem-solving skills.

My action plan:

1. **Set daily learning goals:** Spend dedicated time every day learning Java, SQL, Spring Boot, and other relevant technologies.

2. **Practise consistently:** Write code regularly, solve coding problems, and work on practical projects instead of focusing only on theoretical knowledge.

3. **Learn from mistakes:** When my code fails or produces errors, try to understand the root cause rather than getting frustrated or giving up.

4. **Accept feedback:** Take feedback from mentors, trainers, and teammates positively and use it to improve my coding skills.

5. **Step outside my comfort zone:** Take up challenging assignments and explore unfamiliar technologies instead of avoiding difficult tasks.

6. **Track my progress:** Review my daily learning, identify areas where I need improvement, and work towards achieving small, measurable goals.

7. **Take ownership:** Be responsible for my learning and career development instead of blaming external circumstances for my lack of progress.

8. **Stay consistent and patient:** Understand that becoming a good software developer takes time, continuous effort, and practical experience.

My goal is to focus on continuous improvement rather than immediate results, learn from every challenge, and become a better software developer every day.
